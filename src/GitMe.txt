In this example, I will show you how we used MCP servers to correlate findings with GitLab and identify how to mitigate the issues that were picked up. Recently, developers came to me and asked me to check why DefectDojo was still picking up these findings after a scan. The dev team advised that they had already been mitigated and even shared evidence by providing the JAR file. So they had proof that the issues were mitigated, even though DefectDojo was still showing an older version. I had previously installed MCP servers for DefectDojo and GitLab and used them to investigate why this commit was still showing these issues even though the team claimed they had been resolved. Looking at the exact prompt I gave to DefectDojo, I asked it to check using MCP why DefectDojo was still flagging these findings even though the team said they were mitigated. It then ran a full scan in DefectDojo to search for those findings. It was also trying to determine if there were any past findings related to this that had already been mitigated and how they were resolved. It came up with some recommendations and also checked the GitLab repository. It identified exactly where in the repository this old version was still being referenced. It then found several older versions that the team needed to update to ensure DefectDojo was picking up the latest version, and the issue was resolved.



The way MCPs work is that they can dig much deeper, especially when working with large datasets, reaching places we can’t easily access unless we run multiple queries that have to be constructed and correlated across different tools like GitLab, GitLab commits, and DefectDojo findings. Without MCP, this could have taken us hours—around six hours—of writing scripts and trying to identify exactly which part of the code still had issues. We would also have had to clone the repository from GitLab. However, MCP can connect directly to GitLab, work with the repository, and check which commits actually fixed the issue. As a result, we were able to complete this in less than five minutes. As you can see, some of the queries ran in about two seconds, while others took six seconds. This reduced the troubleshooting time from roughly six hours to less than five minutes.
