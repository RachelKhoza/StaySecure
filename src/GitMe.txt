1. No Incident Classification Criteria (Pre-P4)
The doc assumes the incident is already classified as P4, but there's no guidance on how to classify it as P4 vs. P3/P5. Without this, teams may misclassify and bypass this entire SOP.
Recommendation: Add a brief classification decision tree or criteria table at the top of the SOP Appendix.

2. No Evidence Retention Policy / Duration
Section 6 says "evidence retention is mandatory (logs, configs, timelines)" but specifies no retention period. HIPAA requires defined retention windows.
Recommendation: Specify minimum retention (e.g., 6 years for HIPAA compliance) and where evidence must be stored (e.g., IHPSAC Jira attachments, secure cloud storage).

3. CAPA Completion SLA is Missing
The RCA & CAPA Execution section (Section 4) defines when CAPA is required but gives no SLA for when it must be completed. The SLA table covers incident resolution, not CAPA delivery.
Recommendation: Add a CAPA completion target per severity (e.g., High: 30 days, Medium: 60 days).

4. No Escalation Path if Privacy Officer is Unresponsive
Privacy notification goes to PrivacyOfficer@iqvia.com, but there's no secondary contact or escalation timeline if there's no acknowledgment.
Recommendation: Add a backup contact and a response SLA (e.g., "If no acknowledgment within 4 hours, escalate to [backup]").

🟡 Process Gaps
5. Step 2 (Risk Assessment) Has No Scoring Framework
The four assessment dimensions (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability, Likelihood) are listed, but there's no scoring methodology. Two analysts could reach different conclusions.
Recommendation: Add a simple 1–3 or 1–5 scoring scale per dimension, with a total threshold that determines Privacy engagement or CAPA requirement.

6. No False Positive / Duplicate Incident Process
There's no guidance on what happens if an investigation determines the incident was a false positive or a duplicate of an existing ticket.
Recommendation: Add a brief closure path for false positives, including who approves the closure and whether RCA is still required.

7. Third-Party / Vendor Incidents Lack a Defined Path
SME Engagement (Step 4) mentions "Vendors or third parties (if applicable)" but the Corporate Escalation section only covers internal CISO/Privacy flows. Vendor incidents (e.g., a third-party SaaS handling ePHI) may have BAA notification obligations.
Recommendation: Add a sub-section in Section 2 covering Business Associate Agreement (BAA) notification triggers and who owns vendor communication.

8. No Defined "Investigation Timeline" Standard
Step 1 of Security Intake says "establish investigation timeline" but gives no standard baseline. Teams will set inconsistent timelines.
Recommendation: Align investigation milestones to the SLA table — e.g., initial findings within 1 business day for High, containment validation within 4 hours for Critical.

🟢 Enhancement Opportunities
9. Add a Decision Flow Diagram
The document is text-heavy. A single flowchart covering: Incident Intake → Severity Classification → Privacy Trigger? → CAPA Required? → Closure would dramatically improve usability, especially for new team members.
10. Versioning & Review Cadence
There's no document version number, effective date, or review schedule. For a HIPAA-regulated document, this is an audit risk.
Recommendation: Add a header block: Version | Effective Date | Next Review Date | Document Owner.
11. Link Between Policy Doc and SOP Appendix
The main policy (Security Finding Reporting, Response & Governance) and the SOP Appendix are in the same document but feel disconnected. The policy's Section 3 (Mandatory Actions) should explicitly reference the SOP Appendix by name/section.
